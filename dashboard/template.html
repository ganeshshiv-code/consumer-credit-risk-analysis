Risk climbs, price falls

Three attributes carry significant risk that the pricing engine does not see: income verification in prime grades, loan purpose, and outright home ownership. Where they stack, the coupon moves the wrong way.

03

The verification paradox

Income verification is supposed to reduce risk. In prime grades it predicts the opposite, and in subprime it predicts nothing at all.

Not / source verified Income verified Wilson 95% interval

Grade A: 2.56% vs 0.47% — 5.4x the risk for 13bps more coupon (Fisher exact p=0.0006). Verification × credit-band interaction: odds ratio 3.3, p=0.0007. As a standalone main effect, verification is not significant (p=0.13) — the two opposing effects cancel.

Why this happens. Verification is not randomly assigned; the platform chooses whom to verify. In the prime band, being asked to document income is a signal that something in the application did not reconcile on its own. The verification outcome is clean — which is why the loan is graded A. The verification trigger is the information, and the grade never sees it.

What it is worth, and whether it holds

Declining Tier 1 and repricing Tier 2 lifts net yield by 138bps. The two inputs that are genuinely uncertain — how many repriced borrowers walk, and how much premium to charge — are yours to move.

Does the ranking survive the assumptions?

Roll-to-charge-off rates flexed from optimistic to very severe.

Loss levels move with the assumptions, as they should. The tier ordering — Core > Tier 2 > Tier 1 — held in all 16 scenarios tested, including LGD, funding, servicing and the lifetime anchor moved one at a time.

Debt-to-income points the wrong way

The lowest-DTI quintile impairs at twice the rate of the middle.

Odds ratio 0.98 per point, p=0.0019 — significant, and inverted. The likely reading is that low measured DTI here is a thin credit file rather than financial strength. This becomes a warning against the intuitive recommendation, not a recommendation.

Data. Real LendingClub loan-level originations, January–March 2018, published as loans_full_schema by OpenIntro and mirrored in Rdatasets. 10,000 loans, 55 fields. No values are simulated.

Method. DuckDB for portfolio arithmetic, Python (scipy, statsmodels) for significance testing and multivariate control. Every figure on this page is generated from the database by python/06_dashboard_data.py — nothing is typed in by hand.

Limitations. One vintage in a benign macro quarter, observed at four months. Absolute loss levels depend on the roll-rate and seasoning assumptions; segment rankings do not. Applicants the platform declined are not observable.
